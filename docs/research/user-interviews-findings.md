## Interview 1: Clinic Director / Owner

**Profile:** Dr. Budi Santoso, DDS — Owner of "Klinik Gigi Senyum Sehat"
- 5 branches across Jakarta (Kelapa Gading, Pluit, Kemang, BSD, Cibubur)
- 8 doctors, 15 staff total
- Operating 7 years

### Opening
**Q1. Tell me about your clinic network.**

> "We started with one clinic in Kelapa Gading 7 years ago. Now we have 5 branches. Each branch has 1-2 doctors, depending on location. Kelapa Gading is the busiest — 3 doctors there. We treat around 400-500 patients per month across all branches."

### Current State — Multi-Branch Visibility

**Q2. When was the last time you needed to check performance across all branches? What did you do?**

> "Every Monday morning. I open WhatsApp and message each branch manager: 'Berapa pasien minggu lalu? Berapa revenue?' Then I wait for them to check their Excel and reply. Sometimes they reply at 10 AM, sometimes 2 PM. If I need the data urgently, I have to call them."

**Q3. Walk me through how you currently track: (a) total appointments, (b) revenue per branch, (c) staff attendance.**

> "(a) Appointments: Each branch has an Excel file. At the end of the week, the receptionist sends me a screenshot or forwards the file. I manually add them up in my own Excel.
> 
> (b) Revenue: Same — they send me total cash + transfer per week. I keep a master sheet. Sometimes the numbers don't match what the accountant records, and we have to trace back where the error was.
> 
> (c) Attendance: We have fingerprint machines at each branch, but I don't check them regularly. Only if there's a dispute about who was present. The device has a USB export, but it's tedious — the admin has to plug in a USB stick and email me the file."

**Q4. How long does it take you to get those numbers right now?**

> "If I need a weekly report — 1-2 hours to collect data from all branches and consolidate. If I need monthly reports for the accountant — half a day, because there are always errors I need to fix."

**Q5. What report do you check every Monday morning?**

> "Total appointments, total revenue, and no-show rate. I want to know if any branch is underperforming. But I have to ask manually — there's no automatic dashboard."

### Pain Points

**Q6. What's the biggest operational headache with running multiple branches?**

> "**Visibility.** I don't know what's happening in real-time. If a branch has a problem — equipment breakdown, staff conflict, patient complaint — I only find out hours later when someone calls me. I want to see everything in one place without asking."

**Q7. Have you ever had a situation where Branch A was fully booked but Branch B had empty slots? How did you find out?**

> "Yes! This happens all the time. A patient calls Kelapa Gading, we're full, but I don't know if Pluit (5 km away) has availability. The receptionist says 'Sorry, we're full this week' and the patient goes to a competitor. We're losing patients because we can't see across branches."

**Q8. How do branch managers currently report to you?**

> "WhatsApp for daily updates. Weekly Excel report. Monthly meeting in person."

**Q9. What decision have you delayed because you didn't have the right data?**

> "Hiring a new doctor. I wanted to know if BSD branch is busy enough to justify a second doctor, but I don't have historical data on appointment density by time of day. So I keep delaying the hire because I'm not sure."

### Current Tools & Workarounds

**Q10. What software or tools do you use today?**

> "- Excel for everything (appointments, revenue tracking)
> - Jurnal.id for accounting (but I enter the data manually from Excel)
> - WhatsApp for communication
> - Website: I paid a freelancer to make a WordPress site 3 years ago. It's outdated — we never update the doctor schedule or promotions.
> - Fingerprint device for attendance (Solution brand)
> - That's it."

**Q11. If you use Excel — who maintains it? How often does it break?**

> "Each receptionist maintains their branch's Excel. They break constantly — formulas get deleted, someone overwrites the wrong cell, the file gets corrupted. Last month, Pluit lost 2 weeks of appointment data because the file corrupted and they didn't have a backup."

**Q12. Have you tried any clinic management software before?**

> "Yes, 3 years ago we tried Klinik+ (the hospital management system). It was horrible. Too complicated — designed for hospitals, not small dental clinics. The UI looked like Windows 98. Training took weeks. After 2 months, the staff begged me to go back to Excel, so we did. Wasted IDR 5 million on setup."

### Buying Criteria

**Q13. If you could have one dashboard — what 5 numbers would you want to see?**

> "1. Total appointments today (all branches)
> 2. Revenue today vs yesterday
> 3. No-show rate this week
> 4. Which doctors are fully booked vs underutilized
> 5. Low stock alerts (if we're running out of supplies)"

**Q14. Would you pay per-branch, per-user, or flat monthly? What feels fair?**

> "Flat monthly for all branches. IDR 2-3 million per month is reasonable if it saves me 10+ hours per week. Per-user pricing feels unfair — if I hire more staff, my cost goes up? That's penalizing growth."

**Q15. What would make you say 'this is worth paying for' in the first demo?**

> "Show me a **real-time dashboard** with all 5 branches' data updating automatically. If I can see today's appointments across all branches without asking anyone — I'll sign immediately. Also, if patients can book online and I see those bookings in real-time — that's a game-changer."

### Closing

**Q16. Would you want all 4 modules at once, or start with just booking?**

> "Start with **website + booking** (GROW + CONNECT). That's the immediate pain. Operations module (shift scheduling, inventory) is nice to have but not urgent. Analytics module — I'd want that once I have booking data flowing in."

**Q17. Who else should I talk to?**

> "Talk to Sari, my branch manager at Kelapa Gading — she deals with the daily chaos. And Rina, the receptionist at Pluit — she's the one answering 50 WhatsApp messages per day from patients trying to book."

## Interview 2: Branch Manager

**Profile:** Sari Wijaya — Branch Manager, Klinik Gigi Senyum Sehat Kelapa Gading
- Managing 3 doctors, 3 staff
- 3 years in role

### Opening
**Q1. How long have you been managing this branch?**

> "3 years. I manage 3 doctors, 2 receptionists, and 1 assistant. This is our busiest branch — we do 150-200 appointments per month."

### Daily Operations

**Q2. Walk me through your typical Monday morning — what's the first thing you check?**

> "I check WhatsApp — did any staff call in sick? Then I check the appointment Excel to see how many bookings we have today. If it's a busy day (10+ appointments), I make sure both receptionists are present. If someone is sick, I have to call in backup or handle reception myself."

**Q3. How do you create the weekly staff schedule? How far in advance?**

> "I make it on Friday for the next week. I write it in a notebook, then type it into an Excel file, then send it to the staff WhatsApp group. Doctors tell me their availability, and I try to match staff shifts to when doctors are present. It takes about 1 hour every Friday."

**Q4. When a staff member calls in sick, how do you handle coverage?**

> "Panic. I call other staff to see if they can come in early or stay late. If no one is available, I have to cover reception myself, which means I can't do my other work (ordering supplies, handling complaints, etc.)."

**Q5. How do you know when you're running low on supplies?**

> "I walk to the supply room and look. If something is low, I add it to my shopping list (a note on my phone). Then on Monday, I WhatsApp Dr. Budi to ask for approval to order. After he approves, I order via WhatsApp to our supplier."

### Communication & Reporting

**Q6. How often do you report to the director? What do you send?**

> "Every Monday: I send him the weekly appointment count, total revenue, and any issues (equipment breakdown, patient complaints). He also asks me random questions during the week like 'How many appointments does Dr. Andi have on Wednesday?' and I have to check Excel and reply."

**Q7. What requests need director approval?**

> "- Ordering supplies over IDR 1 million
> - Hiring staff (even temporary)
> - Giving discounts to patients
> - Changing the schedule (if a doctor wants to take leave)"

**Q8. How do you communicate with receptionists and doctors?**

> "WhatsApp group. We have 3 groups: (1) All staff, (2) Doctors only, (3) Kelapa Gading branch. It's messy — important messages get lost in the chat."

### Pain Points

**Q9. What takes up the most time in your week that feels like it shouldn't?**

> "**Collecting data for Dr. Budi's Monday report.** I have to open 3 Excel files (appointments, revenue, inventory), count things manually, and format it nicely in a WhatsApp message. It takes 30-40 minutes every Monday. If there was a button that says 'Export weekly report,' that would save so much time."

**Q10. Have you ever ordered supplies and realized another branch had extra stock?**

> "Yes! Last month we ordered 10 boxes of gloves, then I found out Pluit had 15 boxes sitting unused. If we had shared inventory visibility, we could have just transferred stock instead of ordering."

**Q11. What's the worst thing that can go wrong in a shift?**

> "Double-booking. It happens when two receptionists enter appointments at the same time in different Excel files, or when they forget to check the paper book before confirming a booking. The patient shows up, but the slot is already taken by another patient. Very embarrassing."

### Closing

**Q12. If software could automate one part of your job, what would you pick?**

> "**Weekly reporting.** Just give me a button that generates the numbers Dr. Budi needs, and I'll love you forever."

## Interview 3: Receptionist / Front Desk

**Profile:** Rina Kartika — Receptionist, Klinik Gigi Senyum Sehat Pluit
- 2 years in role
- Handles phone + WhatsApp + walk-in bookings

### Opening
**Q1. How long have you worked here? Do you handle walk-ins only, or also phone/WA bookings?**

> "2 years. I handle everything — walk-ins, phone calls, WhatsApp messages. We get 10-15 WhatsApp messages per day asking for appointments. WhatsApp is busiest — people don't like calling anymore."

### Booking Flow

**Q2. Walk me through what happens when a patient calls to book.**

> "1. I ask: Name, phone number, which doctor they want (or I suggest one), when they want to come.
> 2. I check the paper appointment book (we still use it as backup) and the Excel file to see if that slot is free.
> 3. If it's free, I write their name in the book, then type it into Excel.
> 4. I give them the appointment time and remind them to come 10 minutes early.
> 5. If they ask to pay via transfer, I send them our bank account number via WhatsApp."

**Q3. How do you avoid double-booking?**

> "I check both the paper book and Excel before confirming. But sometimes I forget, especially if I'm handling 2 patients at the same time (one on the phone, one at the desk). Then we get double-booked."

**Q4. Do patients book via WhatsApp? How do you handle that?**

> "Yes, most patients book via WhatsApp now. They send a message like: 'Kak, bisa booking untuk hari Rabu jam 2 siang?'
> 
> I check the schedule, then reply: 'Bisa kak, dokter Andi available. Nama lengkap dan nomor telepon?' They give me the info, I enter it into Excel and the paper book, then I confirm via WhatsApp: 'Sudah di-booking ya kak, Rabu 14:00 dengan dokter Andi. Jangan lupa datang tepat waktu 😊'
> 
> It takes 2-3 messages back and forth per booking."

**Q5. How often do you get booking requests outside clinic hours?**

> "All the time! People message at 8 PM, 10 PM, Sunday morning. I try to reply the next morning, but sometimes I forget because the message gets buried under other chats. I think we lose patients because of this — they book elsewhere by the time I reply."

**Q6. Have you ever double-booked by accident?**

> "Yes, 3 times this month. Once because I was busy with a walk-in patient and didn't update Excel immediately after a phone booking. Another receptionist (we have 2) took a WhatsApp booking for the same slot 10 minutes later. The patient showed up and we had to reschedule one of them. Very awkward."

### Check-In & Daily Flow

**Q7. Show me how you track today's appointments.**

> "I print the Excel schedule every morning — one page per doctor. When a patient arrives, I check their name on the paper, cross it off, and write the time they checked in. Then later I update Excel with 'Hadir' (attended) or 'Tidak hadir' (no-show)."

**Q8. When a patient arrives, what's the check-in process?**

> "I ask their name, find them on the printed list, confirm the doctor and time. If they're a returning patient, I pull their paper file from the cabinet (we have 500+ files — takes 2-3 minutes to find sometimes). If they're new, I create a new file. Then I tell them to wait, and I call the doctor via WhatsApp: 'Dok, pasien sudah datang.'"

**Q9. How do you handle no-shows?**

> "I mark them as 'Tidak hadir' in Excel. I don't call them — no time for that. Maybe 20-30% of patients no-show on Mondays (after the weekend). It's frustrating because we could have given that slot to someone else."

**Q10. If a doctor is running late, how do you inform waiting patients?**

> "I tell them verbally: 'Maaf kak, dokternya sedikit terlambat, mungkin 15 menit lagi.' No formal queue system. Patients just sit and wait. Sometimes they get annoyed and ask how much longer — I have no way to know."

### Pain Points

**Q11. What's the most frustrating part of managing appointments?**

> "**Double-booking and last-minute changes.** When a doctor suddenly says 'I need to leave early today' at 11 AM, I have to call 5 patients to reschedule. I don't have their phone numbers easily accessible — I have to search Excel row by row. It takes 30 minutes."

**Q12. Do patients cancel or reschedule often?**

> "Yes, 10-15% cancel or reschedule. They WhatsApp me: 'Kak, hari ini ga bisa, reschedule ke Jumat bisa?' I have to find their booking, delete it, move it to Friday, then confirm. It's manual work."

**Q13. If you could change one thing about the current booking process?**

> "Let patients book themselves online. Like GoJek or Traveloka — they pick the time, we just confirm. I spend 3-4 hours per day just replying to booking WhatsApp messages. If they could book online, I could focus on helping patients who are actually here at the clinic."

### Closing

**Q14. Would you trust an online booking form, or prefer to confirm every booking manually?**

> "I'd trust it if: (1) It checks availability in real-time so no double-booking, (2) I can still cancel or reschedule if needed, (3) I get a notification when someone books so I'm not surprised. If those 3 things are there, I'd love it — it would cut my workload in half."

## Interview 4: Doctor

**Profile:** Dr. Andi Pratama, DDS — General Dentist
- 5 years practicing
- Works at 2 branches (Kelapa Gading 4 days/week, Pluit 1 day/week)

### Opening
**Q1. How many years practicing? Multiple branches?**

> "5 years. I work at Kelapa Gading (Monday-Thursday) and Pluit (Friday). I prefer working at one branch, but Dr. Budi asked me to help at Pluit because they're short on doctors."

### Schedule & Availability

**Q2. How far in advance do you decide your weekly schedule?**

> "I tell Sari (branch manager) my schedule for the next month at the end of each month. I have some fixed commitments — Thursday afternoons I teach at the university, so I'm only available mornings. If something comes up (family event, training), I WhatsApp Sari at least 1 week before."

**Q3. If you need to block a day (leave, sick, training), how do you communicate that?**

> "I WhatsApp Sari: 'Kak, tanggal 15 aku ga bisa ya, ada acara keluarga.' She says OK, then she has to manually reschedule all my appointments for that day. I feel bad because it's a lot of work for her and the receptionist."

**Q4. Do you ever feel overbooked or under-utilized?**

> "**Overbooked on Mondays and Fridays** — we have 15-20 patients and I'm exhausted by the end of the day. **Under-utilized on Wednesdays** — sometimes only 5-6 patients. I don't know why. Maybe people don't book on Wednesdays? If I could see the pattern, I'd adjust my schedule."

**Q5. Do patients request you specifically?**

> "Yes, maybe 40% of patients ask for me by name (returning patients). The receptionist usually accommodates if my schedule allows. New patients just get whoever is available."

### Patient Information

**Q6. Before a consultation, what info do you want about the patient?**

> "- Name, age
> - Why they're here (cleaning, toothache, cavity, cosmetic)
> - Previous visit history (when was the last visit, what treatment)
> - Allergies or medical conditions (diabetes, heart disease — affects anesthesia)
> 
> Right now, I only get name and reason. The receptionist verbally tells me if the patient has been here before, but I don't have written records easily accessible."

**Q7. Do you have access to previous visit history?**

> "Paper files only. The receptionist hands me the file when the patient checks in. If the patient went to a different branch last time, I don't have that file — it's at the other branch. So I have to ask the patient: 'When was your last visit? What treatment?' They don't always remember."

**Q8. If a patient has been to a different branch, can you see that record?**

> "No. Each branch has separate paper files. If I really need the info, I have to call the other branch and ask them to read the file to me over the phone. It's rare that I do this — usually I just treat based on what the patient tells me."

### Pain Points

**Q9. What's the most annoying operational issue?**

> "**Last-minute schedule changes and not knowing my patient load in advance.** Sometimes I arrive at the clinic and there are 18 patients booked — I wish I had known the night before so I could mentally prepare. Other times I expect a busy day and only 4 patients show up."

**Q10. If you could change one thing about scheduling?**

> "Let me see my schedule on my phone. Right now I have to ask the receptionist every day: 'Berapa pasien hari ini?' If there was an app where I could check my appointments for the next week, that would be perfect."

### Closing

**Q11. Would you use a web portal to manage your own availability?**

> "Yes, **if it's simple.** I don't want to spend 30 minutes learning a complicated system. If it's like Google Calendar — click a slot to block it, done — I'd use it. But if it's complicated like that Klinik+ system we tried before, forget it."

## Interview 5: Patient

**Profile:** Ibu Dewi — 34 years old, mother of 2
- Lives in Kelapa Gading
- Visits dental clinic 2x per year (routine cleaning + occasional cavity treatment)

### Opening
**Q1. When was your last dental visit?**

> "2 months ago — routine cleaning at Klinik Gigi Senyum Sehat Kelapa Gading. I've been going there for 3 years."

### Discovery

**Q2. How did you find that clinic?**

> "Google search: 'klinik gigi Kelapa Gading.' I saw them on Google Maps, read the reviews (4.5 stars), and it's only 10 minutes from my house. I also checked their Instagram to see the clinic photos — it looked clean and modern."

**Q3. What information did you look for before deciding to go?**

> "- Location (must be close to home or my office)
> - Price (I wanted to know if cleaning costs IDR 200K or IDR 500K — couldn't find price on their website, had to call)
> - Doctor background (I prefer experienced doctors, but their website doesn't show doctor profiles)
> - Parking (important! Many clinics in Jakarta have no parking)
> - Reviews (I trust Google reviews more than the clinic's own website)"

**Q4. Did you visit their website or social media first?**

> "I visited the website — it's very basic. Just the address, phone number, and a few photos. No doctor profiles, no price list, no online booking. I had to call to book."

### Booking Experience

**Q5. How did you book your appointment?**

> "I called first time. The receptionist answered, I asked: 'Bisa booking untuk hari Sabtu pagi?' She said yes, asked for my name and phone number, confirmed the time. Simple, but I had to call during office hours (they close at 5 PM, I work until 6 PM, so it was difficult to call).
> 
> Second time, I booked via WhatsApp — much easier. I sent a message at 8 PM, they replied the next morning at 9 AM. But it's still back-and-forth — not instant like booking a Grab."

**Q6. If you called or used WhatsApp — how long did it take to get a confirmed slot?**

> "WhatsApp: 12 hours (I messaged at night, they replied next morning). Phone call: 3 minutes (instant if they pick up, but sometimes line busy)."

**Q7. Did you receive a reminder before the appointment?**

> "The receptionist sent me a WhatsApp reminder 1 day before: 'Kak Dewi, besok jam 10 pagi appointment dengan dr. Andi ya, jangan lupa 😊' Very helpful! Without that reminder, I might have forgotten."

**Q8. Have you ever booked and forgotten to go?**

> "Once, yes — I booked 2 weeks in advance, got the reminder 1 day before, but on the actual day I got distracted with work and completely forgot. If they had sent another reminder 2 hours before the appointment, that would have helped."

### Visit Experience

**Q9. When you arrived, how long did you wait?**

> "10-15 minutes usually. Sometimes 30 minutes if the previous patient's treatment took longer. The receptionist doesn't tell me how long I'll wait — I just sit and hope."

**Q10. Did the staff know why you were there, or did you have to re-explain?**

> "They asked when I arrived: 'Mau cleaning atau ada keluhan?' Even though I told them on WhatsApp when I booked. I had to repeat myself. Then the doctor asked again: 'Apa keluhannya hari ini?' I said 'Cleaning saja, dok' — third time repeating. A bit annoying."

### Closing

**Q11. If you could book online as easily as ordering food, would you prefer that?**

> "**100% yes.** If I could:
> 1. Open the clinic website or app
> 2. See which doctors are available on Saturday morning
> 3. Pick a time slot
> 4. Enter my name and phone
> 5. Get instant confirmation + reminder
> 
> That would be perfect. I hate calling during office hours or waiting 12 hours for WhatsApp reply. Make it like Halodoc or Alodokter — those apps are so easy."

## Findings Summary

### Top 5 Pain Points (Ranked by Frequency)

1. **No real-time multi-branch visibility** (Director) — spends 1-2 hours per week collecting data manually
2. **Double-booking** (Receptionist) — happens 3+ times per month due to manual coordination
3. **Manual booking process** (Receptionist + Patient) — 3-4 hours per day handling booking messages
4. **No patient self-service** (Patient) — expects online booking like other services (Grab, Halodoc)
5. **Lost patients due to lack of cross-branch visibility** (Director) — Branch A full, Branch B empty, patient goes to competitor

### Current Tools in Use

| Tool | Usage | Pain Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Excel** | Appointments, revenue, inventory tracking | Corrupts frequently, no real-time sync, error-prone |
| **WhatsApp** | Patient booking, internal communication | Messages get lost, manual back-and-forth takes hours |
| **Paper book** | Backup appointment log | Redundant work, not searchable |
| **Jurnal.id** | Accounting | Manual data entry from Excel (not integrated) |
| **Fingerprint device** | Staff attendance | Data stuck on device, USB export tedious |
| **WordPress website** | Public clinic info | Outdated, no booking widget, no doctor profiles |

### Willingness to Pay

- **Director:** IDR 2-3M/month for all branches (flat rate preferred over per-seat)
- **Trigger to buy:** Real-time dashboard + online patient booking
- **Dealbreaker:** If system is too complicated (Klinik+ experience was traumatic)

### Critical KPIs (from Director)

1. Total appointments today (all branches)
2. Revenue today vs yesterday
3. No-show rate this week
4. Doctor utilization (fully booked vs underutilized)
5. Low stock alerts

### Booking Flow Bottlenecks

- **Receptionist:** 3-4 hours/day handling booking WhatsApp messages
- **Patient:** 12-hour delay for WhatsApp confirmation (messages at night, reply next morning)
- **Manual coordination:** Check paper book + Excel + ask doctor = 2-3 minutes per booking

### No-Show Frequency

- **20-30% on Mondays** (post-weekend)
- **10-15% other days**
- **Solution patients want:** 2-hour advance reminder (1-day reminder not enough)

### Doctor Schedule Communication Friction

- Doctor WhatsApp manager → manager manually reschedules all appointments for that day → receptionist calls patients
- **Takes 30+ minutes per doctor leave day**

### Patient History Access

- **Paper files only** — separate per branch
- **Cross-branch patients:** No shared records, doctor has to ask patient to repeat history

### Discovery Channels (Patient)

1. **Google Maps** (primary) — search "klinik gigi [area]"
2. **Google reviews** (trust signal)
3. **Instagram** (check photos, vibe)
4. **Website** (basic info, but no booking)
5. **Word of mouth** (secondary)

### Preferred Booking Method (Patient)

1. **WhatsApp** (current favorite — 70% of bookings)
2. **Online form/app** (patient wants this — like Halodoc)
3. **Phone call** (declining — office hours conflict)

### No-Show Reasons (Patient)

- Forgot (booked 2 weeks in advance, 1-day reminder not enough)
- Work conflict (last-minute meeting)
- Traffic (Jakarta traffic unpredictable)

## Go/No-Go Decision: **GO**

### Validation of MVP Assumptions

| Assumption | Validated? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Excel/WA is the real competitor | ✅ Yes | All personas use Excel + WA, hate it but don't know alternatives |
| Multi-branch visibility is top pain | ✅ Yes | Director spends 1-2 hours/week manually collecting data |
| Receptionists want online booking | ✅ Yes | "Would cut my workload in half" |
| Patients expect online booking | ✅ Yes | "Make it like Halodoc or Alodokter" |
| Pricing ceiling IDR 2-3M/month | ✅ Yes | Director willing to pay if value is clear |
| Double-booking is frequent problem | ✅ Yes | 3+ times per month at one branch |
| WhatsApp is king | ✅ Yes | 70% of bookings via WhatsApp |

### What Changed from Original Plan

1. **Online booking is even more critical than thought** — receptionist spends 3-4 hours/day (50% of shift) handling manual bookings
2. **Cross-branch patient referral** is a hidden pain — Director didn't realize they're losing patients until asked directly
3. **Doctor wants mobile access** to view schedule — add this to CONNECT module UX (responsive mobile view priority)
4. **2-hour advance reminder** needed (not just 1-day) — add to CONNECT notification flow

### MVP Scope Adjustment

Keep as-is from PROJECT_PLAN.md:
- ✅ GROW: CMS website with doctor/service/location
- ✅ CONNECT: Multi-branch booking + WA reminders + check-in
- ✅ OPERATE: Deferred to Phase 2 (validated — inventory/shift scheduling is nice-to-have, not urgent)
- ✅ INTELLIGENCE: Deferred until CONNECT data exists

Add to CONNECT:
- [ ] **2-hour advance reminder** (in addition to 1-day reminder)
- [ ] **Mobile-optimized doctor view** (doctor checks schedule on phone)
- [ ] **Cross-branch availability search** (if Branch A full, show Branch B slots)
